# The Line

*Keeping the peace, and keeping your word.* Session 3 of 3 in [Craft Under Pressure](../).

## Recommended prerequisites

[Ground Truth](../01-ground-truth/) and [What's Mine](../02-whats-mine/). This session assumes both: the vocabulary to say what is happening, and the calm to say it without heat.

## What this session does

Converts two sessions of clarity into sentences a person can say in a meeting.

This is the only session in the series that is genuinely prescriptive, and it is deliberately last: by now the room has the words and has had four weeks of practice at the cheap moves. It is also the session most likely to be heard as an indictment by anyone who has been on the other side of an optimistic report, which is why "The recorded decision" is framed as protecting the person who made the call rather than exposing them.

**The relief it delivers:** agency — something sayable on Monday that is not insubordination.

## Reading Assignment

**Attendees:** *The Clean Coder*, ch. 1 "Professionalism", ch. 2 "Saying No", ch. 3 "Saying Yes". Three short chapters.

**Presenters:** add *Extreme Programming Explained*, ch. 4 "Values" and ch. 5 "Principles".

This is the only part of the four-book canon that carries weight in this series. Those four books cover *how to do the work*; they are nearly silent on what to do when the work is being described dishonestly, and three chapters is the extent of it. The README says so rather than implying otherwise.

## The Practice

**One Sentence** — twenty minutes, three passes per scenario, in pairs.

1. Name the conflict — structural language, no names, no motives.
2. Separate control — which part is yours to decide?
3. State the boundary — one sentence, out loud, to a partner playing the other party.

The constraint is the exercise: **no adjectives, no apology, no "just"**. What is left is usually a sentence somebody can act on.

### The scenarios

| | Scenario | The failure to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| A | The report that isn't true — you are reviewing it, not writing it | The room wants to talk about the author |
| B | The estimate told to halve | Agreeing to three while privately planning six |
| C | The thing asked to ship silently | Playing the hero instead of recording the decision |

Scenario C is conditional. Run it only if A and B went well; the boundary there is rarely "refuse", it is "get the decision written down with a name and a date".

## Before you run it

- **"The line in the sand"** is the prescriptive centre of the whole series. Defend it without hedging; the asymmetry between the columns is the argument.
- **"Leaving is legitimate"** is not optional. Without it the deck argues for martyrdom, and somebody in the room is close enough to that line for it to matter.
- The **commitment at the end is not collected**. In a small in-person group the instinct is to make it public, and a public commitment turns a professional boundary into a performance for colleagues.
- Keep the **confidentiality promise** made in session 1. Say here that nothing from the room will be attributable in the write-up.

## After the series

The written piece is assembled from all three sessions, not just this one: friction points named, trade-offs argued, sentences that landed. No quotes, no attribution. The arc gives it the structure — the gap, what's mine, the line.

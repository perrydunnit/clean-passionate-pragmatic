# What's Mine

*Control, force, and where the energy goes.* Session 2 of 3 in [Craft Under Pressure](../).

## Recommended prerequisites

[Ground Truth](../01-ground-truth/) — this session assumes the vocabulary from it, and opens by asking what happened to the sentence people carried out.

No philosophical background needed. The reading is short and stands on its own.

## What this session does

Takes the pressure the room named in session 1 and separates it into the part that is theirs to decide and the part they are only allowed to have opinions about. Then gives them the older vocabulary for why that separation is hard.

The highest-relief session of the three. Most of the anxiety in the room is effort spent on decisions nobody handed them, and being told by Epictetus and by a diagram that the effort was never going to work is a permission slip, not a defeat.

**The relief it delivers:** permission to put it down.

## Reading Assignment

**Attendees:** the *Enchiridion* of Epictetus in full — about an hour, and the best return on an hour anywhere in this series. Then *Tao Te Ching* ch. 8, 22, 43, 78, and *The Art of War* ch. 1, 3, 10.

**Presenters:** add *The Heart of the Buddha's Teaching*, ch. 9 "Right View" and ch. 12 "Right Effort".

None of it is about software. That is the point: these are older answers to a question the industry has not answered. The four sources say one thing in four registers, and different registers reach different people — nobody needs all four, they need the one they will not resist.

## The Practice

**The Control Sort** — fifteen minutes over a week of actual work.

- *Mine to decide.* I choose, and the outcome is mine.
- *Mine to influence.* I can move it, and somebody else decides.
- *Not mine.* I have an opinion. That is all I have.

Then two choices, said out loud to a partner: one item in column one to act on, one item in column three to stop carrying. Those two choices are the whole exercise.

## Before you run it

Two slides carry the session and should not be cut to save time:

- **"Water, not force"** — the room will hear it as "just go along with it". That objection is correct to raise and the slide has the answer. Let it run.
- **"When not to spend it"** — the counterweight. Without it the session becomes an argument for endurance.
